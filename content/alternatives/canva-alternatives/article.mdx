Canva Pro isn't the deal it used to be. Depending on your region and whatever promo is running, it lands somewhere between $12.99 and $18 a month if you pay monthly — and that's for one person. The second a teammate needs their own login, you're pushed onto Canva Business, which runs $20-25 per seat. Throw in the fact that a lot of the good templates and stock photos are locked behind Pro anyway, and it makes sense that "canva alternatives" is one of the most searched phrases in this whole category.

So we went through 13 tools people actually switch to and checked what they cost and do right now, not what some 2023 roundup said. There's no single "best" one here — someone leaving Canva because of price wants a completely different tool than someone leaving because Canva feels too limited for actual design work. So this is organized by why you'd want out in the first place.

## How we put this list together

Quick note on methodology, since it affects how much weight to give any of this: this comparison is research-based, not a months-long hands-on trial of all 13 tools. For each one, we checked:

- **Official pricing pages**, pulled the same week this was published (July 2026), since most of these tools change pricing or plan structure at least once a year
- **What's actually included at each tier** — free plan limits, watermarks, credit caps, seat types — not just the headline price
- **Verified user reviews** on G2 and Capterra, weighted toward recent reviews, to catch complaints that don't show up on a vendor's own marketing page
- **Product changelogs and company announcements** for anything that changed the calculus recently — the Affinity acquisition being the clearest example below

We didn't rank these on a points system or run every tool through an identical test brief. If you want a "which is objectively #1" verdict, this isn't that. It's closer to: here's what each tool is actually for, what it costs today, and who regrets picking it.

## Why people actually leave Canva

A few reasons keep coming up:

- The free plan is fine for the occasional graphic, but premium templates, stock assets, and the brand kit tools all sit behind a paywall.
- If you've used Illustrator or Photoshop, Canva can start to feel thin — there's no real multi-layer editing, no vector precision to speak of.
- It's built around templates first. Great when you're in a hurry, less great when you want something that doesn't look like every third Instagram post.
- It's cloud-only. No real offline mode, which is annoying if your internet is unreliable or you need local files.

## If you just want a Canva-like tool that's cheaper or does more

### 1. Adobe Express

**Adobe Express** is the one most people actually put up against Canva Pro head-to-head, and it's not a bad fight. You get drag-and-drop editing plus Adobe Firefly baked in for AI generation and background removal. The free plan gets you limited AI credits and around 100,000 templates; Premium is $9.99/month and unlocks 200 million-plus Adobe Stock assets, the full Adobe Fonts library, and 250 generative credits a month. If you already pay for Creative Cloud for Photoshop or Illustrator, there's a decent chance Express Premium is already included — worth checking before you pay for it twice.

*Best for: Freelancers and social managers who want Adobe-quality assets without learning Photoshop, or anyone already in the Adobe ecosystem.* *Skip if: You rely on Canva's sheer template volume — Express's library is a fraction of Canva's, and its team approval workflows are weaker.*

### 2. VistaCreate

**VistaCreate** used to be called Crello, and it's the closest thing to a Canva clone that doesn't cost Canva money. What's surprising is how much the free Starter plan actually gives you — over 100,000 templates, a million-plus royalty-free assets, animated templates, 10GB storage, and direct posting to Instagram, Facebook, and Pinterest, with no watermark. Pro is $10/month if you pay annually (closer to $13 month-to-month) and adds a 70-million-asset premium library plus unlimited brand kits.

*Best for: Small businesses and creators who publish often and don't want Canva Pro's price for what's often a small feature gap.* *Skip if: You need polish over speed — the editor can lag under heavy use, and asset search is clunkier than Canva's.*

### 3. Figma

**Figma** is a different animal entirely — it's not really a Canva competitor so much as a tool people graduate to once Canva stops being enough. It's built for UI/UX and product design, but a lot of brand and marketing teams have adopted it because the vector engine is just more capable, and true multiplayer editing (several people in the same file, live) works better than anything Canva has. Free covers you up to 3 files, fine for messing around, not for real work. Professional runs about $12/editor/month if you commit annually, more like $16 month-to-month — and Figma's pricing gets genuinely confusing because it splits seats into "Full," "Dev," and "Collab" types at different rates.

*Best for: Teams that need a shared component library or design system, and eventual developer handoff.* *Skip if: You just need an Instagram post by 3pm — the learning curve isn't worth it for one-off graphics.*

## If your problem is presentations or data, not social graphics

Canva's animation and data tools are pretty basic — that's the most common complaint from people who use it for anything beyond static posts. Two tools fill that gap:

### 4. Visme

**Visme** does live charts that actually pull from a spreadsheet, exports to real PowerPoint files, and on higher tiers exports SCORM packages for e-learning — none of which Canva touches. The free Basic plan works but slaps a watermark on everything. Paid plans scale up from there, with Pro landing around $25/month annually (roughly $297/year), meaningfully more than Canva Pro for one person.

*Best for: Marketing and sales teams building data-backed decks or reports, not just static graphics.* *Skip if: Budget is the priority — you're paying for depth here, not savings.*

### 5. Piktochart

**Piktochart** is the simpler, cheaper sibling — it does one thing, turning data into infographics and reports, without the learning curve Visme has. Free plan has basic templates; Pro is about $14/member/month.

*Best for: Teams that regularly publish reports and one-pagers and don't need Visme's broader feature set.* *Skip if: You need presentation depth beyond infographics — Piktochart is intentionally narrow.*

## If you just need something fast

Not everyone needs depth. Sometimes you need a graphic in two minutes and you're done.

### 6. Snappa

**Snappa** strips out almost everything except templates and a big stock library (5 million-plus images) plus one-click background removal. Free plan caps you at 3 downloads a month, which will annoy you quickly; Pro is $10/month annually ($15 month-to-month), Team is $20/month annually for up to 5 people.

*Best for: Solo marketers who want to open a tool, grab a template, and export in under two minutes.* *Skip if: You need more than basic graphics — there's no infographic or presentation layer here.*

### 7. Stencil

**Stencil** does the same job with a slightly different focus — built for bloggers who need a header image or a quick social graphic, nothing more. Over 5 million stock photos, 1,350-plus templates. Free plan is limited on downloads; Pro runs about $9/month annually, Unlimited around $12.

*Best for: Bloggers and solo content creators who need "make image for post," repeatedly, with zero learning curve.* *Skip if: Your needs will grow — you'll likely outgrow Stencil once you need more than headers and quick social posts.*

## The best free options, if price is the whole reason you're here

A few tools genuinely don't make you pay anything meaningful to get real use out of them.

VistaCreate's free tier is probably the most generous of the bunch — full animated templates, over a million assets, no watermark, no credit card. Adobe Express's free plan is worth a look too if AI generation matters to you, since it includes limited Firefly credits.

### 8. Affinity Suite

Here's the one most people miss, though: **Affinity**, the Designer/Photo/Publisher suite, used to cost around $70 as a one-time purchase and was already considered one of the best Photoshop alternatives around. Canva bought Affinity's parent company, Serif, back in 2024, and in 2025 made the entire suite completely free. So there's now a genuinely professional-grade vector and photo editor, at zero cost, owned by the same company that makes Canva. It's a strange twist, but a good one if you need real editing power without paying for it.

*Best for: Anyone who wants pro-level design tools without a subscription, full stop.* *Skip if: You need real-time team collaboration — Affinity is single-user, desktop-based software, not a browser tool built for teams.*

### 9. GIMP

And if you want something that isn't browser-based at all, **GIMP** has been free and open-source for years — full layers, over 1,000 plugins, a real scripting system for automating repetitive work.

*Best for: People who want a permanent, zero-cost desktop editor and don't mind a learning curve.* *Skip if: You want something intuitive on day one — GIMP's interface takes real time to get comfortable with.*

## "I need something more like Photoshop, not more like Canva"

A chunk of people searching for Canva alternatives aren't actually looking for another Canva — they've outgrown templates entirely and want real layer-based editing. If that's you, Canva-style tools are the wrong direction. A few honest options instead:

### 10. Photopea

**Photopea** runs in your browser and opens native .PSD, .XD, and Sketch files. The interface is close enough to Photoshop's that tutorials made for Photoshop mostly work step-for-step here. Free with ads, $5/month without them.

### 11. Affinity Photo

**Affinity Photo**, now free as noted above, is the strongest all-around replacement for retouching and compositing — non-destructive editing, RAW support, and it's noticeably fast thanks to GPU acceleration. No subscription, ever.

### 12. Adobe Photoshop

If what you actually need is Photoshop's AI tools — Generative Fill, Neural Filters — none of the free options fully replicate that yet. At that point the honest answer is just to keep paying for **Adobe Photoshop**, currently around $22.99/month on its own.

## Decision tree

```text
Tight budget, mostly social graphics?
  → VistaCreate or Snappa

Already paying for Creative Cloud?
  → Adobe Express (check if it's already included first)

Handing designs to developers / need a design system?
  → Figma

Reports or data-heavy presentations?
  → Visme (deeper) or Piktochart (simpler, cheaper)

Want real editing power, no subscription?
  → Affinity (full suite) or Photopea (browser, PSD support)

Need a blog header image in the next five minutes?
  → Stencil or Snappa
```
